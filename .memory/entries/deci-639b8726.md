The four core-corner stacks are grid columns that gen_medium_office placed on the wall corners, then left in the column group so direct design would not see one 24 m panel (ACI 318-11 §13.6.1.2). This office is an FEA flat plate, and direct design is already refused by the lobby span (§13.6.1.3), so that reason does not apply.

In the solved v4 model the shell delivers the slab to the wall lines. Those sticks are sized, but only to the small end of the catalog (21×21, 6 #8). At the ground story the column check demand is about 45–55 kN (ratio about 0.01) and punching demand is 4–6 kN, against a stored tributary of 50–53 m². A real corner column on the same floor is 28×28 with a punching ratio of 0.91.

The tributary split still gives a column at a cell corner its half-bay square when that square is larger than the region closer to the column than to the wall. A column standing on the wall loses that nearest-support contest everywhere, so the square wins and the wall's complement along that edge is empty. That is why the core walls between those columns have no area of their own, and why the corner reads as a column. The lobby wall is the exception: its ends are not columns, so it keeps the 1.25 m strip past the squares.

The user: walls are line supports and are loaded as walls. Columns that are not sized or detailed as columns do not exist. These four stacks should leave the column group, and the slab beside those walls should be the wall's. v4 still contains the sticks. Do not rerun the office design until asked. The picture keeps the corner squares until the columns are dropped and the scene is rebuilt.
