Agreed with the user on 2026-11-17 after the 30 in qualification waffle grew solid heads of 15-20 bays per quadrant around 4-5 in ribs, so most of the slab became a flat slab.

Goal: size waffle and joist floors for nonstandard bays (triangles, parallelograms, general polygons). Standard dome forms are a benchmark only, not the design space.

1. Each rib piece between intersections is sized on its own by the beam MIP, with its own width and depth (prismatic). Grouping can impose uniformity where wanted.
2. Depth may step at a node between adjacent pieces, up to a step limit (a setting). This needs detailing for bottom bars anchored across a step.
3. Solid head (hybrid): punching shear sets a minimum head around each column, which may be deeper than the ribs (like a drop panel). A bay whose void becomes too small to form, because the ribs around it widened, joins the head and never reopens. A cap on the solid fraction makes the design report failure instead of becoming a flat slab.
4. A rib piece at a head face carries its shear on concrete alone (V_u <= phi V_c, ACI 318-11 §11.11.1.2 / §8.13.8) by default; stirrup credit there is a setting. The piece upsizes first; the head grows only when no catalog section works.
5. Open question to settle in the corpus before coding: how ACI 318-11 §8.13.2 (depth <= 3.5 x minimum rib width) applies when depth varies along a rib.

Supersedes the head-growth-only response in deci-a53796b6 for oversized heads; deci-98b1d8e2 (rib shear demand fed to the beam sizer with stirrups) stays reverted.
