Scoping note from 2026-11-07. Neither analysis exists. Member forces come from an elastic solve. Story P-delta (Wilson & Habibullah, `story_geometric_stiffness`) softens the rigid-diaphragm masters and leaves every member elastic.

## Leaning columns

A leaning column adds axial load to the story and adds no lateral stiffness. Gravity columns today are fixed-fixed frame elements stacked on one plan point. `Column.braced` only skips sway magnification and defaults to true. Beam edges can be pinned; column edges cannot.

What is already true:
- `story_axial_profiles` puts every frame member's vertical force into the story geometric stiffness, so a column's compression already softens the masters.
- AISC `compute_B2` takes story load from all columns and story stiffness from the lateral system only. The steel checker does not call it.

What it would take:
1. A leaning flag and bending releases at the floors. Stacked columns that share a free joint still act as a cantilever. The pin is what removes the story stiffness.
2. Leave the story stiffness alone. A pinned column still delivers axial force to the cut. Test: a frame column sets Pe, a leaning column adds a known P, and the drift matches 1/(1−ΣP/Pe). A building with no leaning column stays bit-identical.
3. A minimum lateral load on gravity-only combinations, or story drift is zero and there is nothing to amplify. Read the factor from AISC (notional load) and the ACI nonsway test before encoding it.
4. Design the leaning column for axial plus its own P-δ. Pinned ends have essentially no end moment, so the story δs magnifier does not size it. Concrete: k = 1.0 and the nonsway member magnifier, with its Pu still inside story ΣPu. Steel: B1 on the column, B2 on the lateral system.

Settle this before pinning: a rigid diaphragm releases θx and θy, so only columns and walls restrain the joint, and pinning gravity columns is the right model when walls are the lateral system. Where the floor is a shell, the slab already restrains the column, and a pin would drop that stiffness.

A physically out-of-plumb column (the inclined-wall sense, todo-d243bfdf) is a different change: two plan points, a horizontal gravity thrust, and tributary and punching keyed to one (x, y).

## Plastic analysis

Plastic section modulus and Mp are strengths checked after the elastic solve. `solve_nonlinear!` runs Newton–Raphson with tangent K + Kg and balances restoring force K·u. The elastic stiffness never drops, so the iteration settles on the elastic displacement. The building analysis does not call it.

An elastic-plastic hinge analysis that fits this frame model:
1. Solve the elastic frame, including the story geometric stiffness.
2. When an end moment reaches the plastic moment reduced for axial load, release that end the way a pin release works and keep the plastic moment as a constant end action.
3. Re-solve. Update the geometric stiffness from the new axial forces.
4. The load factor at which the residual stiffness becomes singular is the collapse load. A leaning column never hinges. It stays pinned and axial, and its compression keeps reducing that collapse load.

Steel compact shapes already carry Zx and Mp, so the hinge moment has a source. Read AISC's bracing, rotation-capacity, and second-order requirements before encoding them.

Concrete does not go through that mechanism. The code allows a limited redistribution of the elastic moments, with its own percentage and section limits. Those limits have to be read before they are encoded. A concrete hinge that holds Mp would overstate the strength.
