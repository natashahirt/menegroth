Final ground-truth on find-d1ef9cef / find-19318fa2. Instrumented diag_grillage_girder_load.jl with (a) per-cell _cell_split_joist_loads probe, (b) total model TributaryLoad geometric area, (c) slab_edge_strips accounting.

Results (topology/r2c1, steel_rib_composite, prepare! + to_asap!):
- Every cell's _cell_split_joist_loads conserves EXACTLY: split_area = gross = cell.area (20/20 cells ratio 1.000; the 15 m^2 diagonal slivers included).
- Total model TributaryLoad geometric area = 495.8 m^2 vs 480.0 floor = 1.033.
- slab_edge_strips: 40 strips, 15.65 m^2 run-area + 0.25 m^2 re-entrant corner excess = 15.90 m^2.
- 480.0 + 15.90 = 495.9 ~= 495.8 model. The 3.3% is ENTIRELY the slab overhanging the column centerlines out to the column faces (slab_edge.jl), which is real load that must be applied. Corner excess is a documented conservative choice (0.25 m^2, negligible).

Conclusion: there is NO tributary over-count / leak. The earlier '1.41x' and '1.03-1.13 per-slab' numbers were measurement artifacts: 1.41 = 1.033 (real edge overhang) x 1.37 (load factor); the 1.03-1.13 came from reading cell_tributary_loads records (which include edge strips / dead+live) rather than the authoritative per-cell split. Nothing to fix in the routing.

The non-physical 1772 kN girder shear on the failed steel_rib_composite run therefore traces solely to the composite + two-way-slab story-shell amplification on the non-standard stacked floor (steel one-way joists made composite with a CIP two-way RC slab), NOT to tributary. Sane systems (steel_deck, concrete_rib, bare_steel) are unaffected.
