2026-11-08. Corpus search (ACI 318-11, ACI 318-19 with commentary, ACI 421.3R-15, ACI 421.1R-99, PCA EB712, fib MC 2010, CRSI economical-RC 2016) found no clause for the effective depth or shear area of a two-way critical section that crosses ribs.

What the codes do say:
- Location: ACI 318-11 §11.11.1.2(b) (page 200) and ACI 318-19 §22.6.4.1(b) place a critical section at d/2 from changes in slab thickness (capitals, drop panels, shear caps). ACI 421.3R-15 §4.4 (page 14) says to check several sections.
- Analogy: PCA EB712 page 539 treats waffle slabs as flat slabs with solid heads acting as drop panels, so the outer check reuses the drop-edge punching path.
- The only numerical rule is spSlab Eq. 2-14, quoted in the tier-3 example sp-de-two-way-waffle-slab-aci318-14: shear area is the rib webs crossing the perimeter, Ac = sum(bw * d). Cite it as that vendor method, not as a code clause.

Chosen checks:
- At the column: full head depth, existing drop-panel punching, unbalanced-moment transfer (§13.5.3 and §11.11.7). No 1.10.
- Outside the head: rectangle d/2 beyond the head edge. Rib-web area. Direct shear only. Vu is the column reaction minus self-weight inside the section. Output states that eccentric shear at this section is not covered by a code clause. No 1.10.
- Each rib leaving the head: one-way shear at d from the head face, through the existing joist shear path. The 1.10 applies here only when §8.13.1–§8.13.3 are met.

The 1.10 itself: ACI 318-11 §8.13.8 (page 121) allows 10 percent above Chapter 11, which includes two-way shear. ACI 318-19 §8.8.1.5 (page 127) narrows it to the one-way values in 22.5. The StructurePoint example applies 1.1 to punching under 318-14. User decision: one-way rib shear only, never punching, so the factor survives a move to 318-19. The two copies of 1.10 in joist_construction.jl (Vc_joist and _joist_vc_factor) are to share one helper and one named constant.

Also chosen, same session: head size starts from an input and grows by whole rib modules when punching fails; NLP carries §8.13.2 and §8.13.3 as constraints and re-checks the snapped section; each head is classified as a drop panel or a shear cap from §13.2.5 / ACI 421.3R §5.4.
