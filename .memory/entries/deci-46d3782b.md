Three limits on walls as slab supports are closed. This narrows deci-bfa4c684, which refused a wall shorter than half the panel edge in direct design. It does not change deci-b34e4300 (punching, Table 9.5(c) ln/30).

One-way shear at a wall (ACI 318-11 §11.11.1.1, page 200). The section is at d from the face, across the wall length. Vu = qu × max(tributary area − L·d, 0). Vc is §11.2.1.1 Eq. (11-3), φ = 0.75. A failure thickens the slab the same way the panel check does. The panel check stays.

Strip split when a wall runs along the span (ACI 318-11 §13.6.4, pages 255–256). A wall longer than half the panel edge and parallel to the span selects the αf1·ℓ2/ℓ1 ≥ 1 row. On the slab edge it also sets βt = 2.5, so §13.6.3.3 Column (4) applies. A wall perpendicular to the span and at least (3/4)ℓ2 long spreads negative moment uniformly across ℓ2 (§13.6.4.3). The slab keeps the whole column-strip moment. §13.6.5.1 would assign 85 percent of that moment to a beam with αf1·ℓ2/ℓ1 ≥ 1; that wall is not proportioned, so §13.6.4.5 leaves the slab portion equal to the column strip. Table 9.5(c) stays ln/30.

A short wall is allowed in direct design (R13.1, page 243). It counts as a support. A step between two columns splits when both pieces lie on the slab; a step across an opening ends at the wall. Columns on both sides are successive spans under §13.6.1.3 (page 252). Flexure from the wall face to the nearest column uses Column (3) at an end and the interior coefficients when columns stand on both sides. Those moments enter the strip envelope. Column demands and punching stay on the columns. Open core floors remain ineligible under §13.6.1.1.
