Corrects find-d1ef9cef. The earlier diagnostic divided applied FACTORED TributaryLoad force by SERVICE pressure x area, so the 1.41 ratio was ~1.36 load factor x ~1.03 geometry, not a 41% double path.

Pressure-independent per-slab AREA check (sum _tributary_area_m2 of each cell's stored loads vs cell.area):
- The four 60 m^2 rectangular bays: ratio exactly 1.000.
- The sixteen 15 m^2 diagonal/sliver cells: 1.06-1.13 (worst slabs 12,15,19,20 at 1.132).
- Overall applied area ~496 m^2 vs 480 m^2 net = ~3.3% over.

Cause of the small real leak: the one-way joist strip is exact only for a rectangle aligned to the joist frame. On the diagonal bays the joists are oblique to the across-joist band axis, so a strip applied over the joist's full length slightly over-covers its sheared band (over-cover ~1/cos(obliquity); 13% => ~28deg). It slips past the conservation gates because the gate's length basis differs from the emitted 3D element length. Minor, conservative; worth a ticket but NOT the cause of the failure.

Real driver of the non-physical girder demand: a clean gravity FRAME solve (no composite) gives a reasonable worst of 356 kN / 1479 kN*m. The failed steel_rib_composite run reported 1772 kN / 2100 kN*m on group 8 (~5x). That amplification comes from the full composite + two-way-slab story-shell model on the joist hybrid, not from the tributary partition. The 10.5h runtime and the shell_deflection_ll failure both trace to that config (steel one-way joists made composite with a cast-in-place two-way RC slab), which is a non-standard stacked floor.
